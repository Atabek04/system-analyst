## 7. Our Platform Concept & Competitive Positioning

> This section defines what we're building, why it's differentiated, and what research says about each feature.

### 7.1 Core Philosophy

**Self-paced + pre-recorded** as the primary delivery mode — not live-only.

Why this wins over live-only (competitors like OTUS, Netology do live-first):
- Student schedule ≠ instructor schedule → live classes lose 30–40% of potential audience
- Fast learners get bored waiting; slow learners fall behind → both churn
- Instructors repeat themselves inconsistently across cohorts (fatigue, off-days, forgotten points)
- Students too shy to ask questions in front of peers (especially "basic" questions) → silent confusion
- Pre-recorded = instructor's best version, always consistent, never tired

**Research confirms**: 94% of learners prefer short, focused lessons. Microlearning (<15 min) consistently outperforms long-form lectures for retention.

But self-paced has a structural problem: **3–8% MOOC completion rate**.
Our fix: keep self-paced video + add every accountability mechanism research says works.

---

### 7.2 Platform Features

#### A. Lesson Design
- **≤15 min per lesson** — microlearning standard (Brilliant.org model); students feel daily progress
- **Interactive checkpoint every 2–3 min** — not passive watching; retrieval practice embedded
- **One concept per lesson** — Zettelkasten principle applied to video curriculum
- Pre-recorded = consistent quality; instructor's best take, always available

#### B. AI Lesson Chatbot
- Embedded on every lesson page — student asks question right inside the lesson, no context switch
- **Course-aware system prompt**: knows the full curriculum, current lesson topic, what's been taught so far, what's coming next
- **Pedagogical guardrail**: if student goes too deep into future topics → AI reminds "this comes in Module X, let's not jump ahead"
- Guides toward understanding, does not just give answers (Khanmigo / Socratic approach)
- Advantage over generic ChatGPT: no hallucination on course content; no "this is not in my knowledge"

*Research backing*: AI tutors that guide > AI tutors that answer. Khanmigo (Khan Academy) validates this model at scale.

#### C. Assessments (after each lesson + after each module)
Multi-format to test different levels of Bloom's Taxonomy:
- **MCQ** (recall — Bloom's L1–L2)
- **Fill-in-the-gaps** (recognition — L2)
- **Written answer** (synthesis — L4–L5)
- **Matching** (understanding — L2–L3)
- **Scenario-based MCQ** — real situation → what would you do? (Bloom's L3–L4, scales like MCQ)

*Research backing*: Scenario-based assessments show "increased analytical skills" and better transfer to real job performance vs. pure MCQ. Formative checks every 5–10 min dramatically outperform end-of-module-only testing.

#### D. AI-Graded Assignments
Human-like artifacts graded by AI — this is the key differentiator:
- **BRD** (Business Requirements Document) for a given case
- **SRS** (FR + NFR) writing
- **User Stories** (INVEST criteria check)
- **BPMN diagrams** — draw and submit; AI reviews structure and logic
- **UML diagrams** — Use Case, Sequence, Class, ERD
- **"What's wrong here?"** tasks — AI shows a flawed artifact, student identifies issues and proposes improved version
- **SQL Sandbox** (LeetCode-style) — real DB, instant feedback, incremental difficulty
- Infinite task generation: AI generates unique scenarios per student → no answer-sharing problem

*Research backing*: Auto-graded coding sandboxes (LeetCode model) work because of immediate feedback, incremental difficulty, and flow state maintenance. AI-generated scenario-based MCQ prevents answer-sharing. AI feedback on open-ended work = viable first-pass (hybrid AI + human review for edge cases).

#### E. AI Stakeholder Simulation & Roleplay
- AI plays a client, product owner, or business stakeholder
- **Intentionally incomplete** — doesn't say everything; student must ask the right questions
- Tests elicitation skills: how do you find the gaps? what do you ask?
- Different domains each time: fintech, e-commerce, healthcare, logistics
- Can be linked to assignment: "interview this stakeholder, then write the User Stories"

*Research backing*: AI roleplay creates psychological safety for high-stakes professional practice (nobody's judging you). Copient.ai validated this model for enterprise sales roleplay. This is early-stage in bootcamp context — **differentiator opportunity**.

#### F. AI Mock Interview
- Voice agent (not text) — simulates real interview pressure and communication
- Gives different cases and domains each session
- Integrated tools during interview:
  - SQL sandbox (live query writing)
  - Artifact reviewer (draw a diagram, AI evaluates it)
  - "What would you do here?" scenario questions
- Immediate feedback after each answer: what was strong, what was missing

*Research backing*: Voice AI interview tools (Yoodli, Skillora, Bossed) show strong early adoption. Voice format forces communication practice — not just recall. "Safe to fail" is the core value.

#### G. Spaced Repetition Flashcards (Anki-style)
- Pre-created cards per lesson by instructors
- After completing a lesson → that lesson's cards are added to student's deck automatically
- SRS algorithm schedules reviews at optimal intervals (Ebbinghaus spacing: 1 day → 3 → 7 → 14...)
- Card types: MCQ, fill-in-the-gap, written recall, diagram recognition
- Each card has a **"Go to lesson"** link → student can review the source video if they can't recall
- AI can explain the card topic briefly if student is still stuck

*Research backing*: Without review, **75% of content is lost within 6 days** (Ebbinghaus). Spaced repetition is the single most evidence-backed memory technique. Duolingo built its entire retention model on this principle. Active recall (flashcards) outperforms passive re-reading by 2–3x on retention tests.

#### H. Weekly Live Sessions (Instructor-Led)
- Not the primary delivery — the accountability and human layer
- Students bring their stuck points and blockers
- Instructor clarifies what AI couldn't answer
- Motivational function: instructor spots students falling behind → proactive outreach
- Analogy, storytelling, emotional support — things AI can't replicate
- Small group (cohort) format → peer learning, social accountability

*Research backing*: Coaching/accountability lifts completion from 3–8% to 70%+. Harvard's online courses saw **85% completion lift** by adding social learning. Even one weekly touchpoint dramatically changes retention outcomes.

#### I. Live Webinars with Expert
- Weekly live sessions — not primary delivery, but the human accountability layer
- Students bring blockers and stuck points; instructor clarifies what AI couldn't answer
- Real-time Q&A removes the "I'll figure it out later" trap that kills self-paced completion
- Instructor spots passive/falling-behind students → proactive outreach → motivation
- Analogy, storytelling, emotional support — things AI cannot replicate
- Format: small cohort group, not a lecture to hundreds

*All major competitors offer this. It's table stakes — not a differentiator, but its absence is a dealbreaker.*

#### J. Curator & Mentor Support Team
- **Curators** — track student progress, send check-ins, answer administrative and motivational questions
- **Career consultants** — CV review, LinkedIn optimization, interview coaching, job search strategy
- **Technical mentors** (optional paid add-on) — 1:1 deep-dive sessions for stuck students
- Response SLA: curators available async within 24h; live sessions weekly
- This is the human safety net that keeps completion rates above 70%

*Research confirms: coaching/accountability is the single biggest lever on completion rate — from 3–8% (pure self-paced) to 70%+.*

#### K. Career Center & Job Placement Support
- CV review and feedback aligned to SA job descriptions
- LinkedIn profile optimization
- Mock interviews (human) + AI mock interview (built into platform)
- Job board partnerships or curated vacancy list
- Alumni network access — students who got hired help the next cohort
- Certificate recognized by hiring partners

*All major Russian bootcamps include this. Without it, students don't trust that the course leads to a job — enrollment drops.*

#### L. Gamification
- **XP after each lesson** — visible progress; compound motivation
- **Levels** — milestone markers ("you just hit Middle level")
- **Badges** — tied to meaningful achievements, not trivial actions (e.g., "First BPMN completed", "SQL streak 7 days")
- **Streak with freeze protection** — Duolingo data: streak freeze → 48% longer streaks; 3x daily return
- **Relative leaderboard** — small pools (~30 students), weekly reset; never global absolute ranking (demoralizes bottom 50%)
- **Friends** — see friends' progress → social accountability without shame
- **Multiplayer quiz battles** — Kahoot-style; questions from completed modules; first correct answer wins health points; team format reduces shame while keeping excitement

*Research backing*: Early achievement on Day 1 → 13-point retention gap at 14 days (Duolingo A/B test). Gamification meta-analysis effect size g=0.822 (strong). Global leaderboards backfire — small pool relative ranking is the evidence-based design. Streak architecture is Duolingo's single biggest retention lever.

### 8.3 Pricing criteria / takeaways

- **Full-program floor:** ~110 000 ₽ (Eduson Basic, Yandex). **Typical street price:** ~93 000–120 000 ₽ (after standard discount). **Ceiling:** ~215 000 ₽ sticker (Skillbox), OTUS specialization ≈ 232k+ equivalent.
- **Installment is the real headline price.** Big platforms compete on **~3 000–5 000 ₽/mo over 24–36 months**, not lump sum. OTUS is the outlier (~12 000–17 600 ₽/mo) because its programs run 6–13 months with no 36-month stretch.
- **Mini-course band:** ~29 900–54 900 ₽; typical ~45 000–50 000 ₽ per ~2.5-month focused topic. Cheapest discrete units in the market.
- **Discount theater:** Skillbox/GeekBrains/Eduson run permanent "−50%" anchors — list price is fiction; the discounted number is the real price.
- **Job guarantee splits the market:** Skillbox + GeekBrains advertise "work or money back"; OTUS, Yandex, systemanalyst.life offer help only, no guarantee.

> **Implication for our pricing:** A full AI-native program priced **~99 000–129 000 ₽** (installment ~3 500–4 500 ₽/mo ×36) sits credibly mid-market — below Skillbox/Netology sticker, above the bare floor, justified by the AI feature moat. Per-topic mini-courses anchor at systemanalyst.life's **~45 000–50 000 ₽**. Decide lump-sum vs. installment-first marketing early — the market sells the monthly number.
