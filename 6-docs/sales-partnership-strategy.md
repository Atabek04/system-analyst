# Sales-Marketing Partnership — Strategy, Term Sheet & Draft Agreement

> **For:** Solo founder, System Analyst training course
> **Decision:** Bring on a partner who owns 100% of marketing & sales, founder owns product & delivery.
> **TL;DR recommendation:** Do it — but restructure from a flat 70/30 marriage into a **performance-earned, founder-banked, exit-able** arrangement. Sign nothing until the terms in Part C are in writing.

---

## Assumptions (flag if wrong — they change the recommendation)

1. Course is **built and validated** — paying students or strong signal, not just content sitting in Obsidian.
2. This is a **high-ticket cohort/course** (>$300/seat), not a $29 self-serve product.
3. Partner is a **person/small shop**, not an agency with its own brand.
4. You're **pre-scale** — under ~100 students total.
5. Course is delivered in **Russian to a CIS/regional market** — matters for who "owns" the channel.

---

# Part A — Situation Analysis

**The model is right for you. The flat split and the silence around the details are the problems.**

You have the thing that's hard to build (a validated curriculum) and lack the thing that's easy to rent (distribution). A revenue-share partner solves your real constraint without burning runway. Correct strategy. But:

### Pros
- Zero fixed cost → no revenue needed before you get sales help. Critical for a solo founder.
- Partner paid only on results → no salary risk.
- You stay in product flow → course quality is the moat; protect your attention.

### Cons
- **You go blind on the customer.** If the partner owns lead gen → outreach → closing → support intake, you have no direct line to the student. Optimizing a course in the dark is fatal in EdTech.
- **30% is co-founder pricing for contractor work.** Pure distribution is worth 20–25% unless they bring a captive audience.
- **Single point of failure.** Outsource the *capability* (not just the task) → if they stall, you have no sales and no sales muscle of your own.

### Risks
- **Legal:** undefined IP & data ownership; blanket exclusivity with no exit; partner registering brand/domain/ad accounts in their name.
- **Financial:** "revenue" undefined (gross vs. net of ad spend vs. refunds); ad spend funded by whom; payout before refund window closes.
- **Operational:** support intake with partner → churn signals never reach you; no performance floor → dead-weight partner you can't remove.

# Part C — Recommended Structure (the opinionated answer)

**Do the partnership. Turn 70/30-flat into a performance ratchet you control.**

### The split — CAC-based ratchet, not flat
> ⚠️ **Correction to the original "30% is too generous" take.** At the recommended 109 000 ₽ price *with the partner funding ads from their share*, 30% nets the partner only ~3–18k ₽/sale after CAC. That is thin, not generous. The ratchet must step down on **measured CAC falling** (proof the brand is doing the selling), NOT on a raw student count — otherwise you cut the partner's pay before your brand can carry the load and they walk. See [pricing-strategy.md](pricing-strategy.md) §unit-economics.

- **Launch (high CAC, no brand):** **35%** to partner. They need the upside while CAC is high and volume is low.
- **Step to 30%:** only once blended CAC falls **below ~15 000 ₽** — proof the brand/organic is contributing.
- **Step to 25%:** only at real scale where referral/organic leads dominate and CAC is low.
- **All splits on revenue NET of refunds, chargebacks & processor fees. Partner funds advertising out of their share.**
- If the partner **can't fund ads** → they're a freelancer, not a partner. Switch to a flat **20% commission**, you keep ad control.

### Non-negotiable terms
1. **IP** — all curriculum, brand, content, roadmap owned exclusively by you. Partner gets a marketing license, nothing more.
2. **Payment account** — students pay into **your** processor. You remit the partner's share **monthly, net-30 after the refund window closes** (not at purchase).
3. **Customer data** — you own the list & CRM. Partner gets access, not ownership. On termination, access ends; data stays with you.
4. **Assets in your name** — domain, ad accounts, landing pages, social handles registered to you; partner operates, doesn't own.
5. **Performance floor** — minimum paid students/month (set from your baseline). Two consecutive misses → split reverts or agreement suspends.
6. **Exclusivity** — non-exclusive for first 90 days; afterward, channel-/geo-limited exclusivity **only while** the floor is met.
7. **Termination for convenience** — either party, 30-day notice, no cause.
8. **Non-solicit** — 12 months post-termination: no competing SA course, no poaching your students.
9. **Price authority** — you set prices; discounts need your written sign-off.
10. **Audit rights** — you can inspect sales & ad records quarterly.

### First 90 days
- **Days 1–7:** Sign the agreement. Onboard no leads until signed. Open *your* payment processor & ad accounts; grant partner operator access.
- **Days 8–14:** Define support routing (partner does pre-sale + tier-1 only; delivery/quality issues to you within 24h; you get read access to all tickets). Set the floor from your real baseline. Stand up a shared dashboard (leads, sales, refunds, tickets — live).
- **Days 15–75:** Run the trial at 35%. Weekly 30-min sync: pipeline, CAC, objections, churn reasons. *Listen for why people don't buy — that's your roadmap.*
- **Days 76–90:** Review vs. the floor. Hit → convert to steady-state split + channel exclusivity. Miss → renegotiate or exit on the 30-day clause.

# Part E — Draft Partner Agreement (starting point — have a lawyer review)

> ⚠️ Template, not legal advice. Adapt to your jurisdiction and have counsel review before signing.

```
SALES & MARKETING PARTNERSHIP AGREEMENT

This Agreement is made on [DATE] between:
  (1) [FOUNDER FULL NAME / ENTITY], "Founder"; and
  (2) [PARTNER FULL NAME / ENTITY], "Partner".

1. PURPOSE
   Founder owns and delivers the System Analyst training course (the
   "Product"). Partner provides marketing and sales services to generate
   paid enrollments. This Agreement governs that relationship.

2. SCOPE OF SERVICES
   2.1 Partner: lead generation, advertising, outreach, sales closing,
       and tier-1 (pre-sale and intake) customer support.
   2.2 Founder: curriculum, content delivery, lesson quality, product
       roadmap, pricing, and tier-2 support (delivery/quality issues).
   2.3 Support routing: Partner forwards all delivery, quality, and
       refund matters to Founder within 24 hours. Founder has read
       access to all support tickets and the CRM.

3. INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY
   3.1 All curriculum, content, brand, trademarks, and roadmap are and
       remain the exclusive property of Founder.
   3.2 Partner receives a limited, revocable, non-transferable license
       to use Founder's brand and materials solely for marketing the
       Product during the Term.
   3.3 On termination, this license ends immediately.

4. OWNERSHIP OF ASSETS & DATA
   4.1 All student/customer data, the CRM, and the email list are owned
       exclusively by Founder. Partner receives access, not ownership,
       and may not export, copy, or retain such data beyond the Term.
   4.2 The payment processor account, domain, advertising accounts,
       landing pages, and social handles are owned and held in the name
       of Founder. Partner operates them under access granted by Founder.

5. REVENUE SHARE & PAYMENT
   5.1 "Net Revenue" = gross student payments received, less refunds,
       chargebacks, and payment-processor fees.
   5.2 Partner's share of Net Revenue, stepped by measured blended CAC:
        (a) Launch / CAC at or above [15 000] RUB: 35%
        (b) Once blended CAC falls below [15 000] RUB for [2] consecutive
            months: 30%
        (c) Once blended CAC falls below [8 000] RUB for [2] consecutive
            months (organic/referral-dominated): 25%
       "Blended CAC" = total advertising spend / total paid enrollments
       in the measured month, audited per Section 11.
   5.3 Partner funds all advertising spend from Partner's own share;
       Founder bears no advertising cost.
   5.4 Students pay into Founder's payment account. Founder remits
       Partner's share monthly, net-30 days after the close of the
       applicable refund window.

6. PRICING
   Founder sets all prices. Partner may not offer discounts, payment
   plans, or promotions without Founder's prior written consent.

7. PERFORMANCE FLOOR
   7.1 Partner shall generate at least [N] paid enrollments per calendar
       month.
   7.2 If Partner misses the floor in two consecutive months, Founder
       may, at its option, revert the revenue share to [X%], suspend, or
       terminate this Agreement.

8. EXCLUSIVITY
   8.1 No exclusivity applies during the first 90 days.
   8.2 Thereafter, Partner has exclusive rights limited to
       [CHANNEL / GEOGRAPHY], and only for so long as the performance
       floor in Section 7 is met. Founder retains all other channels.

9. TERM & TERMINATION
   9.1 Term: 90-day initial trial, then continuing month-to-month.
   9.2 Either party may terminate for convenience on 30 days' written
       notice, without cause.
   9.3 Either party may terminate immediately for material breach not
       cured within 14 days of written notice.
   9.4 On termination: Partner's license and all access end; Founder
       retains all data, assets, and IP; Founder pays Partner any share
       earned on payments received before termination, per Section 5.4.

10. NON-SOLICITATION & NON-COMPETE
   10.1 For 12 months after termination, Partner shall not (a) launch,
        operate, or market a competing System Analyst training product,
        nor (b) solicit, contact, or enroll Founder's students or leads.

11. AUDIT
   Founder may inspect Partner's sales, advertising, and lead records
   on reasonable notice, up to once per quarter.

12. CONFIDENTIALITY
   Each party keeps the other's non-public business information
   confidential during and after the Term.

13. INDEPENDENT CONTRACTOR
   Partner is an independent contractor, not an employee, agent, or
   legal partner of Founder. Neither party may bind the other.

14. GOVERNING LAW
   This Agreement is governed by the laws of [JURISDICTION]. Disputes
   resolved by [courts / arbitration] in [LOCATION].

SIGNED:
  Founder: _______________________  Date: __________
  Partner: _______________________  Date: __________
```

## Bottom line
The model fits your constraint exactly — rent distribution, keep attention on the moat. But 70/30-flat with the partner owning payments, data, and support would quietly hand away control of your own business. Make it **performance-earned, founder-banked, and exit-able** → strong move.
