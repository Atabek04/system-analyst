# Role as a Teacher

### You Are NOT a Sage on Stage

**Key Points:**

- **Facilitator role**: Guide discovery, don't just transmit information
- **Your job**: Create conditions for learning, not perform lectures
- Students should talk MORE than you (60/40 rule)
- "I don't know, let's figure it out together" = valid response
- Show vulnerability when stuck on problem

### The Facilitator's Toolkit

**Key Points:**

- **Ask, don't tell**: "What do you think happens here?" before explaining
- **Wait time**: 7-10 seconds after question (count in your head)
- **Redirect**: Student asks you → "What does the group think?"
- **Scaffold**: Break big question into smaller steps
- **Example**: Don't explain normalization → ask "Why is this data duplicated? What problems could that cause?"

### When to Lecture vs Facilitate

**Key Points:**

- **Lecture** (20% of time): New foundational concepts, safety-critical info
- **Facilitate** (80% of time): Application, problem-solving, discussion
- **Pattern**: Mini-lecture (5-10 min) → immediate practice → debrief
- Red flag: You've talked for 15+ min straight → stop and ask question

### The Mixed-Ability Classroom Reality

**Key Points:**

- **Your group will have**:
    - Beginners (never touched SQL)
    - Career changers (some tech background)
    - Junior developers (want BA skills)
    - Someone who already knows 50% of content
- Accept this NOW → design for it

### Differentiation Strategies

**Key Points:**

- **Tiered tasks**: Same problem, three difficulty levels
    - Basic: "Document this feature"
    - Medium: "Document + identify edge cases"
    - Advanced: "Document + design database + API contract"
- Students self-select difficulty (no public announcement)
- Advanced finishers → help others (teaching solidifies learning)

### Pair Work as Equalizer

**Key Points:**

- **Intentional pairing**:
    - Week 1-2: Random (assess levels)
    - Week 3+: Strategic mixing (strong + developing)
    - Never: Same pair twice in a row
- **Strong student benefits**: Explaining = deeper understanding
- **Developing student benefits**: Sees thought process in action
- **Both names on work** = accountability

### Supporting Strugglers Without Slowing Everyone

**Key Points:**

- **Office hours**: Mandatory for students who miss 2+ HW deadlines
- **Pre-work**: Send foundation materials to beginners before class
- **Catch-up resources**: Recorded mini-lectures for basics
- **Don't**: Re-explain in class → boring for advanced students
- **Do**: "I'll send you a video on this, let's move forward"

### Challenging Advanced Students

**Key Points:**

- **Bonus challenges**: "If you finish, try this..." (have 3 ready)
- **TA role**: "Can you help review Aisha's ERD?"
- **Extension questions**: "Now optimize this for 1M users"
- **Early access**: Share next week's problem for early start
- Never say "just wait" → wastes their time and breeds resentment

### The Silent Classroom Problem

**Key Points:**

- **Why it happens**:
    - Too hard (everyone confused)
    - Too easy (everyone bored)
    - Intimidating question phrasing
    - Cultural (waiting for teacher to answer)
    - Just tired
- **Your diagnosis skill** = reading which one it is

### Breaking the Silence: Technique 1 - Lower Stakes

**Key Points:**

- **Bad**: "Who can explain 3NF?" (high stakes, vague)
- **Good**: "Show me with fingers: How confident with 3NF? 1-5"
- **Good**: "Turn to partner: Whisper one example of 3NF violation"
- **Good**: "Everyone write answer on paper, hold up in 30 seconds"
- **Why it works**: Private thinking → shared courage

### Breaking the Silence: Technique 2 - Forced Structure

**Key Points:**

- **Think-Pair-Share** (detailed):
    1. "Think alone: 60 seconds, write your answer"
    2. "Pair: 2 minutes, compare answers"
    3. "Share: Each pair reports ONE difference they found"
- **Why it works**: Everyone participates, even if they don't speak to whole group
- **Introverts love this** (private processing time)

### Breaking the Silence: Technique 3 - Cold Calling (Done Right)

**Key Points:**

- **Announce system upfront**: "I'll call on people randomly - it's practice, not a test"
- **Use tool**: Random name generator on screen (fair, visible)
- **Lifelines**: "You can pass once per session" or "Phone a friend"
- **Positive framing**: "Magira, what's your thinking so far?" (not "Give me the answer")
- **Normalize wrong answers**: "Interesting! Let's explore that..."

### Breaking the Silence: Technique 4 - Physical Movement

**Key Points:**

- **Four Corners**: Assign each corner an answer → students move
- **Stand if you agree**: Gauge opinion quickly
- **Gallery Walk**: Post problems on walls, groups rotate
- **Why it works**: Movement = brain activation, plus shy students hide in groups

### When Engagement is Consistently Low

**Key Points:**

- **Stop and diagnose**: "I notice energy is low. What's going on?"
- **Mid-session survey**: "Text me one word about today's class" (anonymous)
- **Possible issues**:
    - Content relevance unclear (fix: add more "why this matters" stories)
    - Too lecture-heavy (fix: more pair work)
    - Too fast or too slow (fix: adjust pace)
- **Don't**: Keep plowing through planned material → loses everyone

### Managing Your Own Energy

**Key Points:**

- **You set the room's energy**: If you're flat, they're flat
- **Pre-session ritual**: 5 min walk, coffee, favorite song
- **Mid-session recharge**: Their break = your breath moment
- **Warning signs you're burning out**:
    - Dreading sessions
    - Getting annoyed at basic questions
    - Skipping prep work
- **Solution**: Build in slack, ask for help, protect your rest

### Handling Difficult Personalities"

**Key Points:**

- **The Know-It-All**: "Great point - can you help Jamal with his question?"
- **The Disruptor**: Private conversation after class, not public callout
- **The Silent Observer**: Don't force, offer written participation option
- **The Complainer**: "What specifically would improve this?" (make them problem-solve)
- **The Late Submitter**: Clear consequence + one-time grace (not endless exceptions)

### Your Growth as Teacher

**Key Points:**

- **Record yourself**: Video one session, watch it (painful but valuable)
- **Ask for feedback**: "What's one thing I should keep/change?"
- **Talk ratio check**: Use timer to track your speaking vs students
- **Peer observation**: Have another teacher sit in (if possible)
- **Iteration mindset**: Session 8 will be way better than Session 1

### The 'Good Enough' Teaching Standard

**Key Points:**

- **Perfectionism kills**: You'll never have perfect slides, perfect pacing, perfect examples
- **Good enough**:
    - Students learn 70%+ of core concepts
    - They don't dread coming to class
    - Homework gets submitted
    - You're improving each week
- **Focus on**: Relationships + clear explanations > fancy slides

### When You Don't Know the Answer

**Key Points:**

- **Honesty > Faking**: "I don't know" builds trust
- **Follow-through**: "Let me research and tell you next session"
- **Model learning**: "Let's look it up together now"
- **Growth signal**: Shows subject is deep, not surface-level
- **Example**: Student asks about specific DBMS optimization you haven't used → admit it, find answer together

### Slide 60: "Your Teaching Philosophy Statement"

**Key Points:**

- **Write this for yourself** (1 paragraph):
    - What do you believe about how people learn?
    - What's your role in that process?
    - What matters more: covering content or student understanding?
- **Example**: "I believe students learn by doing messy work and getting feedback, not by watching me be perfect. My role is to create safe space for mistakes and push them past comfortable. I'll sacrifice covering extra topics to ensure core concepts stick."
- **Read this before every session**

## **INSTRUCTOR SELF-REFLECTION QUESTIONS (For your journal, not slides)**

### After each session:

- What worked today? (Specific moment/technique)
- What fell flat? (Why do I think that happened?)
- Did I talk too much? (Estimate %)
- Who didn't participate? (Why? What will I try next time?)
- Energy level: Mine ___/10, Room ___/10

### Monthly check:

- Are students improving? (Evidence?)
- Am I improving? (What's different from Week 1?)
- What feedback theme keeps appearing?
- What do I need to learn/practice next?
